# Starfall Miner — revision 0.3

Revision 0.3 migrates the production game to typed C# on Godot 4.7.2 .NET. Simulation, direct-finger input, camera/orientation, presentation, and persistence have distinct responsibilities; the visual and gameplay design below is retained. Native macOS and Android are the delivery targets. There is no C# browser build.

Both activities use portrait orientation. Automatic mining is a quiet view: watch the square planet dissolve, check the wallet, and open ship systems or the extraction log when needed. A full-width Take Control button hands navigation to the pilot without rotating the phone or resizing the desktop window.

The simulation uses fixed world coordinates with a ship-following camera. Mode switches retain the live planet, ship, projectiles, and rewards while camera zoom eases and the replacement HUD ignites. `resources/mode_transition.tres` owns zoom, camera response, and holographic activation timing. There is no orientation wait or mode-driven OS resize. Manual travel has no screen-edge bounds.

Manual mode uses one-thumb held steering. Pressing the world starts movement toward the thumb, dragging the same finger changes direction, and releasing stops. Recompute the world steering point every frame as the camera follows: a stationary held thumb must continue steering beyond the original map location. Moving onto the ship enters a dead zone without dropping finger ownership; moving away resumes. There are no movement/fire sticks or persistent waypoints. A mint steering guide turns gold during mining. Desktop uses hold-left-mouse and motion with the same release-to-stop behavior.

When rocks obstruct the hull corridor, the ship slows down and mines from a safe stand-off, then continues through the cleared space. `resources/manual_flight.tres` owns cruising speed (initially 190 world units/s), extra clearance beyond hull padding (40 world units), approach response (3), and pointer dead zone (24 screen units). Slowing is continuous and movement stays bounded; switching modes does not teleport the ship to enforce clearance.

Held manual mining gives the ship 1.5× damage and 1.6× cadence, or 2.4× nominal weapon output with the same equipment, including auxiliaries. It fires only to clear the held direction. The controlled 60-second starter corridor test mines 86 blocks / 172 CR manually versus 40 / 80 automatically. Actual results depend on steering and terrain; independent drones receive no manual-mode buff. The pilot retains weapon selection, boost, a wallet/extraction log, and return-to-auto controls. Pausing, opening a modal, focus loss, resizing, or changing mode stops movement and clears finger ownership; selecting a weapon preserves held steering. No steering state is serialized.

Planets remain procedural data grids. Starting planets are twice the previous diameter: radius 54 cells, roughly four times the block area. Each later sector increases the authored radius through `54 + 6 × log2(sector)`, with a generated extent that follows the actual radius rather than truncating the terrain to the former 30-cell grid. Existing saved worlds restore exactly and use the larger generation on reaching the next sector or resetting. The scene/resource refactor remains intact: generation settings and appearance are Inspector-editable, while a renderer draws only visible tiles without creating a node for every block.

The mothership's autopilot is a stateful local controller. A first-hit forward cone senses visible rock faces; it never selects targets using the planet center, hidden geology, the global nearest-block list or minimap. Hysteresis holds a visible target; range feedback approaches, brakes and retreats around an 88-unit mining distance; heading alignment gates firing. Near-field separation and two-unit swept collision steps prevent grazing. Contact triggers a local slide/backoff; small inherited overlaps use penetration-reducing movement rather than teleporting. Modest changing lateral corrections create a mining hover, not an orbit. Loss of contact triggers a look-around, then an expanding survey based on odometry. Mode handoff resets only transient controller memory and preserves manually cut terrain. `resources/autopilot_flight.tres` owns sensor/range/acceleration rules.

A read-only Survey minimap occupies the upper-right flight area in both modes, outside held-steering hit regions. It draws a cached coarse terrain raster, live ship/heading/drone markers and the camera footprint. Terrain changes invalidate the raster at a bounded five-Hz cadence; camera transforms do not. Drawing visits only on-screen cells, and explosions/wave fronts gather bounded local cell regions instead of copying the entire enlarged planet each frame.

World appearance now offers only Clean Frontier (Default) and Original/Classic Grid. All other presets, selector pages and rendering branches are removed at the user's request. Their older saved IDs fall back to the default without rewriting terrain, rewards, equipment or progress.

The approved cinematic observatory revision keeps those IDs and grid geometry. Clean Frontier's ordinary rock samples one continuous detailed basalt image across adjacent remaining squares, with hemispheric upper-left lighting, broad relief and exposed blue rims. Texture source dimensions are read once per draw, and source regions are bounded for extended coordinates. The terrain mask is still the actual surviving grid: no image or sphere fills excavated gaps. Damage brightness remains part of each face's tint; resources retain their existing whole-square color codes. Original retains its flat rendering. Texture, gain, detail and lighting strength are presentation parameters in the existing `PlanetVisualStyle` resource, independent of generation and collision.

Material identity is a simple color code on the entire grid square, with no inset stone, unique symbol or etched pattern. Titanium/gold stay still and plain; selected energy cells share the same small fusion-like plasma core and circulating particles. Rare pickup fragments are simple colored motes. Motion is analytic, clipped to the cell, capped at 128 particles and redrawn only as needed (at most 30 Hz when otherwise static), with no per-cell nodes or blur passes. Paused comparison freezes the effect.

Red impact-charge cells ignite on any positive damaging hit. Their aimed preview and bright outward shockwave both use the actual 32-unit/two-cell radius. Damage falls from the center to 45% at the rim, clears nearby shallow ordinary blocks and respects tougher/deep ore. New generation uses a 1.5% charge interval; rare materials never conceal a charge. Chained detonations drain through a queue and bound visual effects to 64 rings. Removal precedes reward publication, so overlapping blasts cannot duplicate pickups or planetary completion. Legacy rare-bearing volatile tiles remain mineral cells rather than disguised charges.

Presets are `PlanetVisualStyle` resources registered on the main scene. A style switch assigns render resources and redraws; it does not regenerate a planet or create a node for each cell. Polygon buffers are reused and offscreen tiles are culled. The chosen stable style ID is an optional v1 save field, with safe default behavior for older saves.

The HUD evokes a cinematic deep-space observatory: midnight navy, white/cyan capital and amber reactor readouts, fine viewport framing, open instrument traces and layered glass controls. Unmodified bundled Inter Regular/Semibold provide proportional labels and a clear heading hierarchy, without relying on system fonts or SF Pro. Labels have no glow outline. Shared nine-slice SVG surfaces are drawn beneath native label ink by `GlassSurface`, with low-opacity nebula detail in panels. Normal edges are dim, hover brightens the surface, presses deepen it and use a stronger white edge, selection adds a persistent accent marker, disabled controls lose their halo, and modal keyboard focus has a distinct violet outline. Flight retains its Tab mode shortcut. Theme shapes live in `resources/hud_theme.tres`; surface references and ink/halo colors live in `resources/hologram_style.tres` and the shared palette.

A static generated nebula with blue/cyan filaments and violet margins replaces the simple cloud bank. A cropped, dim ringed world sits primarily behind the command area, with three fine orbital arcs; 156 deterministic foreground stars add depth. The supplied references informed lighting, materials and composition. Original generated art, its exact prompts and provenance live under `assets/art/`. The distant body has no world coordinates or mining identity. No background movement or parallax is introduced. The background/frame/glass controls have no processing loops, and all decorative Controls ignore input. The HUD gradient preserves contrast over terrain. Existing portrait flight hit areas and camera/navigation mapping remain; wide desktop windows compact the header and cap command width. A wide workshop places its persistent fitted-hull preview and static orbital drydock backdrop beside the paged content. Only content rows scroll on shorter panels; status and exit controls stay fixed, and direct-touch buttons respect ancestor clipping. Phone portrait orientation is retained.

Power-on uses a bounded 3-second neon-starter sequence. Identity strikes first; telemetry, navigation, recovery, loadout and command follow with up to 720 ms stagger. Abrupt on/off strikes, failed ignition, millisecond-scale blackouts and short residual chatter replace smooth luminance pulses. Luminous overshoots diminish as contact stabilizes. A stepped hash produces visible geometric jitter with a 2.4-logical-pixel horizontal budget and smaller vertical motion. Channels return exactly to their home positions and stop processing when settled. Mode status shares the pattern. Workshop ignition runs once on entering a workshop visit; tab switches/reselection, category changes, paging, purchases and resizing do not retrigger it. A later new visit may ignite again.

Glow is a lightweight approximation built from antialiased vector halos around controls and instrument traces, with sharp label ink. A 1 × 128 gradient provides HUD atmosphere; there are no blur passes or additional viewports. Settled modules stop animation processing, telemetry updates at the existing HUD refresh cadence, and the presentation does not change mining, camera coordinates, or input ownership. Real phone profiling remains necessary before claiming a mobile frame-rate target.

Each destroyed block emits two tiny fragments. Both appear to drift and then move toward the ship; one carries the block's credit and energy reward. Resources are awarded once on collection. Pending fragments survive saving and loading. Effects have a bounded particle count and a short lifetime so cascades stay readable.

The progression loop now separates common income from rare crafting materials. Most rocks only pay credits; Titanium, Iridium, and Aether deposits require progressively deeper excavation. Manual tunneling can reach valuable deposits before autopilot finishes stripping the surface, while the credit market offers an alternative. Recipes turn those resources into owned weapons and drone chassis, and credit upgrades improve individual items. There are no drone or player health mechanics.

Fresh saves start with one weaker, single-block Photon lance. Five additional craftable behaviors include a slow expanding Nova front with center-weighted damage, a focused two-block Bore laser, and a continuous Flux beam. Flux beam uses exact ray/grid intersections and time-integrated damage on primary, auxiliary and drone mounts. Held manual aim passes through the pointer with no tile-center snapping or ship-angle lag. Release/cancellation, UI blocking and pause stop it immediately. Nova uses a translucent blue irregular boundary and three outward echoes with reused drawing buffers; its damage front remains unchanged. The mothership can purchase two auxiliary hardpoints at ranks 2/3; all fitted weapons fire concurrently. A weapon instance occupies exactly one mount. Drones each retain one weapon and cruise independent planetary routes. Fleet capacity is three, with bays, blueprints and level ceilings unlocked at sectors 1/2/4/7 or lifetime mining counts 0/150/1,200/5,000, whichever arrives first.

The workshop keeps a live fitted-hull schematic visible across Ship, Fabricator, Fleet and Market, including when upgrading weapons and chassis. Drones require a recall and physical docking before chassis upgrades, mounted-weapon upgrades or refits. Return transfers continue during paused servicing without advancing mining, reward collection or projectile impacts; players redeploy serviced drones. Missions persist across reloads.

Planet health accelerates through a polynomial sector curve while credit richness rises slowly. Deep material deposits add 2.8×/4.5×/7× health on top of depth, kind and sector resistance. The starting wallet affords Power (25 CR) and Fire Rate (20 CR). Each Power level adds 60% base damage (4× at level 5, 13× at level 20); Rate adds 10% cadence for three levels, then 2%; Power also retains its 1.2% passive cadence per level. Early cannon/Scout recipes and Titanium market purchases are affordable, while later upgrade prices accelerate. The separate Rate track persists on the item and respects the same rank ceiling independently; chassis upgrades add 5%/1%, and rank caps limit early equipment growth. Legacy shared levels remain saved, with diminishing rather than exponential contribution. Exact existing tile health and pending rewards remain compatible; the new geology applies when generating a planet. Recipes/prices/ranks live in `resources/crafting_catalog.tres`; geology and sector growth in `resources/planet_generation.tres`. `PROGRESSION.md` records all numbers and the remaining campaign playtesting needs.

Opening pacing is measured through an opt-in deterministic production-simulation purchase plan (`tools/dev.py balance`), rather than inferred only from prices. The current guided plan reaches the first cannon at 40 seconds, an armed Scout at 2:30 and Nova wave at 9:15. Lower material requirements and earlier research make weapons accessible before deep excavation; the first beam costs 115 CR including market material. Three-hour profiles and manual/auto throughput checks supplement regressions; human campaign and phone testing remain necessary. `resources/progression.tres` centralizes the discount and late-investment curves.

A local clearance watchdog handles narrow pockets: try the normal retreat, then after 1.1 seconds without 12 units of positional progress, cut a visible forward first-hit face even when the preferred stand-off cannot be reached. Collision sweeps and speed bounds remain enforced; no global exit/ore map is consulted. Opening room lets ordinary approach/brake/mine behavior resume. Rock hue changes deterministically between sectors in both designs without mutating style resources or special-resource colors.

Weapon output is authored in `resources/progression.tres`: Power gain is 0.6 base damage per level; base outputs for lance/cannon/Ion/Nova/Bore/Flux are 1.1/3.2/3.2/5.8/4.4/6.8 (Flux per second). Stronger Ion/Bore bases preserve their chain/armor roles. The laser carries unused frame energy past destroyed faces with a bounded traversal, retaining exact pointer alignment and occlusion. A single dedicated looping PCM voice follows all active beams, stopping on control cancellation, pause and mute without restarting every simulation frame.
