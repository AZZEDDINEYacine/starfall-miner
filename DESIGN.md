# Starfall Miner — revision 0.3

Revision 0.3 migrates the production game to typed C# on Godot 4.7.2 .NET. Simulation, direct-finger input, camera/orientation, presentation, and persistence have distinct responsibilities; the visual and gameplay design below is retained. Native macOS and Android are the delivery targets. There is no C# browser build.

Both activities use portrait orientation. Automatic mining is a quiet view: watch the square planet dissolve, check the wallet, and open ship systems or the extraction log when needed. A full-width Take Control button hands navigation to the pilot without rotating the phone or resizing the desktop window.

The simulation uses fixed world coordinates with a ship-following camera. Mode switches retain the live planet, ship, projectiles, and rewards while camera zoom eases and the replacement HUD ignites. `resources/mode_transition.tres` owns zoom, camera response, and holographic activation timing. There is no orientation wait or mode-driven OS resize. Manual travel has no screen-edge bounds.

Manual mode uses one-thumb held steering. Pressing the world starts movement toward the thumb, dragging the same finger changes direction, and releasing stops. Recompute the world steering point every frame as the camera follows: a stationary held thumb must continue steering beyond the original map location. Moving onto the ship enters a dead zone without dropping finger ownership; moving away resumes. There are no movement/fire sticks or persistent waypoints. A mint steering guide turns gold during mining. Desktop uses hold-left-mouse and motion with the same release-to-stop behavior.

When rocks obstruct the hull corridor, the ship slows down and mines from a safe stand-off, then continues through the cleared space. `resources/manual_flight.tres` owns cruising speed (initially 190 world units/s), extra clearance beyond hull padding (40 world units), approach response (3), and pointer dead zone (24 screen units). Slowing is continuous and movement stays bounded; switching modes does not teleport the ship to enforce clearance.

Manual firing currently retains a 25% cadence advantage over autopilot, including upgrades and overdrive. It fires only to clear the held direction. Better manual productivity remains a tuning goal rather than a measured guarantee. The pilot retains weapon selection, boost, a wallet/extraction log, and return-to-auto controls. Pausing, opening a modal, focus loss, resizing, or changing mode stops movement and clears finger ownership; selecting a weapon preserves held steering. No steering state is serialized.

Planets remain procedural data grids. The current generation is roughly three times wider with nine times the ore of the original prototype. Existing saved worlds restore exactly and use the larger generation on reaching the next sector or resetting. The scene/resource refactor remains intact: generation settings and appearance are Inspector-editable, while a renderer draws only visible tiles without creating a node for every block.

World appearance can be compared through Pause → Compare World Designs. The first page explicitly lists retained Clean Frontier and Original (the former Classic Grid), alongside the first of eight new restrained technical treatments: Contour, Lithograph, Carbon, Faultline, Monolith, Spectral, Circuit and Radar. These favor simple silhouette, fine instrument marks and muted palettes. Older more decorative designs remain in later pages to preserve saved selections. No visual selection regenerates terrain or changes health, rewards or collision.

Rare material deposits stand out through dark-backed, color-independent silhouettes: three Titanium bars, an Iridium lattice, and an Aether orbital nucleus. Pickups retain the motif. Surface style and mineral identity are separate.

Presets are `PlanetVisualStyle` resources registered on the main scene. A style switch assigns render resources and redraws; it does not regenerate a planet or create a node for each cell. Polygon buffers are reused and offscreen tiles are culled. The chosen stable style ID is an optional v1 save field, with safe default behavior for older saves.

The HUD evokes a holographic ship terminal: unboxed cyan capital and amber reactor readouts, open angular traces, peripheral navigation arcs, live heading/velocity, session extraction text, and chamfered weapon/command controls. A soft gradient keeps text legible over terrain without enclosing the statistics in cards. Color and halo strength are authored in `resources/hologram_style.tres`; the reusable module lives in `scenes/ui/hologram_channel.tscn`.

Power-on uses a bounded 3-second neon-starter sequence. Identity strikes first; telemetry, navigation, recovery, loadout and command follow with up to 720 ms stagger. Abrupt on/off strikes, failed ignition, millisecond-scale blackouts and short residual chatter replace smooth luminance pulses. Luminous overshoots diminish as contact stabilizes. A stepped hash produces visible geometric jitter with a 2.4-logical-pixel horizontal budget and smaller vertical motion. Channels return exactly to their home positions and stop processing when settled. Mode status shares the pattern; modal purchases rebuild content without restarting ignition.

Glow is a lightweight approximation built from antialiased vector halos and font outlines. A 1 × 128 gradient provides atmosphere; there are no blur passes or additional viewports. Settled modules stop animation processing, telemetry updates at the existing HUD refresh cadence, and the presentation does not change mining, camera coordinates, or input ownership. Real phone profiling remains necessary before claiming a mobile frame-rate target.

Each destroyed block emits two tiny fragments. Both appear to drift and then move toward the ship; one carries the block's credit and energy reward. Resources are awarded once on collection. Pending fragments survive saving and loading. Effects have a bounded particle count and a short lifetime so cascades stay readable.

The progression loop now separates common income from rare crafting materials. Most rocks only pay credits; Titanium, Iridium, and Aether deposits require progressively deeper excavation. Manual tunneling can reach valuable deposits before autopilot finishes stripping the surface, while the credit market offers an alternative. Recipes turn those resources into owned weapons and drone chassis, and credit upgrades improve individual items. There are no drone or player health mechanics.

Fresh saves start with one weaker, single-block Photon lance. Four additional craftable behaviors include a slow expanding Nova front with center-weighted damage and a focused two-block Bore laser. The mothership can purchase two auxiliary hardpoints at ranks 2/3; all fitted weapons fire concurrently. A weapon instance occupies exactly one mount. Drones each retain one weapon and cruise independent planetary routes. Fleet capacity is three, with bays, blueprints and level ceilings unlocked at sectors 1/2/4/7.

The workshop keeps a live fitted-hull schematic visible across Ship, Fabricator, Fleet and Market, including when upgrading weapons and chassis. Drones require a recall and physical docking before chassis upgrades, mounted-weapon upgrades or refits. Return transfers continue during paused servicing without advancing mining, reward collection or projectile impacts; players redeploy serviced drones. Missions persist across reloads.

Planet health accelerates through a polynomial sector curve while credit richness rises slowly. Deep material deposits add 2.8×/4.5×/7× health on top of depth, kind and sector resistance. Weapon upgrades add 8% damage and 1.2% cadence per level; chassis upgrades add 5%/1%, and rank caps limit early equipment growth. Legacy shared levels remain saved, with diminishing rather than exponential contribution. Exact existing tile health and pending rewards remain compatible; the new geology applies when generating a planet. Recipes/prices/ranks live in `resources/crafting_catalog.tres`; geology and sector growth in `resources/planet_generation.tres`. `PROGRESSION.md` records all numbers and the remaining campaign playtesting needs.
